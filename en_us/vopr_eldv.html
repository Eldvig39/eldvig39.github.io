Question:  I have a question for you regarding this point. The tooth width is needed for calculation, but reference books, as a rule, only provide the size and shape of the groove. If it is necessary to recalculate the winding data of such an electric motor, is it possible, knowing the internal and external diameter of the iron, the size, shape and number of grooves, to obtain the calculated tooth width.

Answer:  The trick is that the program calculates the induction in the machine's magnetic circuit in three places - in the stator body, in the gap between the stator and the rotor, and the induction in the teeth. If there is no tooth width, it is impossible to calculate the induction in the teeth.
Knowingthe internal and external diameter of the iron, the size, shape and number of grooves, you can get the calculated width of the tooth very approximately, which will reduce the accuracy of the calculation.


Question:  I have a question for you again. In your program, where the ratio of the number of rotor and stator slots is entered, is the value selected from tables or calculated using formulas?

Answer: The ratio of the slots in Eldvig is calculated using formulas from the book by G.K. Gervais "How to calculate the stator winding of an asynchronous motor".
But, since these formulas are for straight slots, we have also inserted tables into the program from which this ratio can be selected.
In addition, Gervais' book was written in the 60s, and since then (as is evident) many tests have been carried out and new data have been developed in practice - which are included in the tables.
Also, in Eldvig, control is carried out using tables. If a message appears that according to current data everything is in order - then it is so.
In addition, all this can be controlled manually using tables.
There are many tables and they are different for different authors. Therefore, the last table from "Design of Electrical Machines", edited by I. P. Kopylov, Moscow, Energiya, 1980, was taken - this is the largest of all the tables.

Question:  Another question, I need to recalculate the engine for 220 so that the starting and working windings work (both were constantly on). How can this be done?

Answer: You can calculate a two-phase motor with permanently connected windings as follows.
1. We consider it as a three-phase motor.
2. In the final form "Summary data" we press the button "Recalculate to single-phase".
3. In the form for entering data for a single-phase electric motor, we enter the number of slots occupied by the working winding - half the number of stator slots, and in the number of slots occupied by the starting winding - the second half of the stator slots.
That is, if the number of stator slots is 24, then we enter 12 in the working window and 12 in the starting window.
4. Press the button "Calculate working winding", select the wire. As a result, we get the wire diameter and the number of turns in the phase of the working winding.
5. We do not count the starting winding.
6. The number of turns in the phase of the working winding is divided into slots, according to the formula:
SpRab= 2*Wкab/Zrab;
where SpRab is the number of turns in the slot of the working winding,
Wrab is the number of turns in the phase of the working winding,
Zrab is the number of slots occupied by the working winding.
The first working winding and the second working winding will be identical and occupy the same number of stator slots.
This is a theory. All that's left for you to do is experiment. But it should all work.

Question:  A question about Eldvig arose - why does it only calculate a hard-wired pitch for single-layer windings?
15 kW motor. On the forum, in another program, they calculated a single-layer winding with a step of 15, Eldvig only gives a step of 18 and one parallel. Or am I doing something wrong?
I am trying to get a result in Eldvig - similar to what was calculated on the forum in other programs.
In the future, I have already realized that it is necessary to check all motors by hardware and winding data before calculating Slavyanka.

Answer: You selected a single-layer coil winding. This is a winding with concentric coils. In this case, the coil pitches will be different.
In the search engine, type "Zhervais G.K. How to calculate the stator winding of an asynchronous motor". There, on page 15, there are recommendations for choosing the winding type. See what Eldvig means by "Concentric coils" and "Coil winding".
For a coil winding, Eldvig calculates a certain conditional pitch (since the coil pitches will be different) using the formula Y = Z / 2 / p. (divide the number of slots by 2 and divide by the number of pole pairs). In fact, this is a diametrical pitch. It does not oblige you to anything and means nothing (but only for windings with concentric coils!).
For single-layer chain windings, the pitches can be changed using the "+1" and "-1" buttons. Pressing the button once sets an even step (not allowed in chain windings). Pressing it again sets an odd shortened step.
For Double-layer windings, the step can be both even and odd - it is also changed by buttons.
In version Eldvig 3.71, we added the "Example" buttons, by clicking on which you can see examples of various windings - coil, chain, double-layer, with a description of the area of ​​application, etc.
As for parallel branches - they can be set in the "Calculation of magnetic and electrical characteristics" form - where inductions are selected.

Question:  I have a question about your Eldvig program. Why does this happen? I enter the same engine dimensions twice, and the program gives me different data twice, why is that? This shouldn't happen!

Answer: If you select different windings each time (for example, chain and double-layer), then the data will be different, because the winding coefficients will be different. If you select different stator interleaf insulation (varnish, paper, no insulation), then the calculation data will also be different.
Keep in mind that in most modern electric motors, the interleaf insulation of the stator sheets is an oxide film on the sheets, and in Eldvig you need to select "No insulation". "Varnish" and "Paper" are in old motors, or in high-power motors.
If you select different winding steps, then everything will also be different, due to the winding coefficients. If you select different current densities in the winding each time, then everything will also be different due to changes in the efficiency values ​​and cos(f), which are calculated in the Eldvig program depending on the full.


Question:  I live in Baku, there are simply no brands of wire that are in the program. Absolutely all the wire is Turkish, it is unknown what standards and specifications it was produced by, there are simply no options.

Answer: In the Eldvig program, wires can be entered manually. You can enter from one to three arbitrary names and diameters of wires at a time. The names can be any. But you need to know the diameters of these wires: with insulation and without insulation.
The number of wires of the same name and diameter is not limited.
For example, you can enter the names and diameters:
Wire Turkish No. 1 diameter 1.02 and 1.05 - 2 wires;
Wire Turkish No. 2 diameter 3.04 and 3.8 - 1 wire;
Wire Turkish No. 3 diameter 0.8 and 0.95 - 15 wires.
In total, there will be 18 wires in the slot.
The program itself will calculate the cross-section of one wire of each brand with and without insulation, the total cross-sections of wires for each brand with and without insulation, and the cross-section of the effective conductor - equal to the sum of all wires of all names and diameters.
In principle, we can add Turkish wires to the program, but we need to know their brands and diameters. If you find a table of Turkish wires - send it to us, we will enter them into the program.


Question:  I want to recalculate the winding wire of a larger diameter to a smaller one, so that the same power remains. I change it, add an additional wire of a smaller diameter, set approximately the same cross-section. The power is aligned with what I need and what should be, and the number of turns does not change. I have tried everything. Can you advise?

Answer:  Everything is correct and should be so. Power does not depend on the number of turns in the slot. The number of turns in the slot is calculated based on the induction you specified in the stator body. i.e. the number of turns the program produces is such that the induction you specified is created in the stator body.
More turns - less induction and fewer turns - more induction. If you need to change the number of turns, then you need to change the induction.
Power depends only on the cross-section of the wire and the current density in this wire.
By changing the cross-section of the wire or the current density, you can change the power. At the same time, with a higher current density, the engine will heat up to a higher temperature. If you change the cross-section of the wire to a larger side with the same current density, the power will increase, and vice versa.
If I understood you correctly, you need to increase the power a little. You add an additional wire of a smaller cross-section, and the winding will be wound in two wires. At the same time, the power calculated by the program suits you. Everything is fine with you. The number of turns will remain the same, both at the same power and at the new one.


Question:  I want to calculate, for example, in 3 wires of 1.25 mm. The number of turns increases in the groove? As I understand, the number of turns per wire remains the same? That is, if 22 turns were specified per groove with a 2.5 mm wire, then with 1.25 mm there will be 66 of them in the slot.

Answer: You replace one 2.5 mm diameter wire with three 1.25 mm diameter wires connected in parallel. There will be 66 wires in the slot, but there will be 22 turns, because these three wires will be connected in parallel. The winding will be wound in "three wires", i.e. three wires will be wound on the template at once, simultaneously from three coils (spools). The output end of each section, the coil will consist of three wires with a diameter of 1.25 mm soldered together.


Question:  Hello, please tell me if it is possible to use the section "Recalculate coils for other voltage" for DC coils. The task is to recalculate a 24 Volt DC coil to 220 Volt DC. Or is this calculation only suitable for alternating current?

Answer: Recalculation of coils for other voltage is performed according to the book "Typical calculations for electrical equipment", Practical manual, 7th edition, revised and supplemented, Moscow, Higher school, 1991. The book says: "Recalculation of coils of direct and alternating current devices for other voltage values ​​is performed according to formulas ..." and the formulas are given. Eldvig calculates exactly according to these formulas. Therefore, in Eldvig you can recalculate coils of both direct and alternating current. But, you cannot recalculate from direct to alternating and vice versa. That is, you can recalculate from direct current only to direct current. And from alternating current only to alternating current. If you rewind like this, write what you get.

Thanks for the answer. Everything worked out. From observations for DC coils, the current density is within the range from 8A per sq.mm to 12-15A per sq.mm. Which is of course higher than the values ​​for electric motors in comparison. But probably it should be so. It works properly.
